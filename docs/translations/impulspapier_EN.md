## News announcement (gematik.de, 23 Sep 2025)

**"Impulse paper for the digital prescription of patient transport"**

Every year in Germany, taxis and rental cars carry out 40 million patient transports, and specialised patient transport
services carry out another 5.6 million. Patient transport is therefore significant for statutory health and long-term-care
insurance. A digitally supported process makes prescribing and carrying out transport easier for patients, their relatives
and transport companies. Our new impulse paper outlines approaches for a digital process.

Application and approval processes in German healthcare are still often paper-heavy. The digitalisation strategy
*"Gemeinsam Digital"* ("Digital Together") aims to simplify them. gematik supports its implementation and analyses care and
administrative processes together with experts. As part of this, a working group developed a digital process for
prescribing travel costs under **§ 60 SGB V** (Social Code Book V). The results are summarised in this paper, which
describes the process steps and derives clear recommendations.

## Cover (p. 1)

**Process transformation in healthcare and nursing care: impulses for implementing a digital prescription of patient transport**

## Introduction (p. 2)

gematik works with practitioners and experts to analyse care and administrative processes in healthcare and nursing care,
in order to find where digitalisation can improve them. In interdisciplinary working groups, participants from the relevant
fields jointly develop concepts for digitally supported processes. The guiding reference is the digitalisation strategy
*"Gemeinsam Digital"* and its goal of using digitalisation to improve processes, and therefore care.

**Between February and July 2025**, a working group designed a digital process for prescribing travel costs under § 60 SGB V.
In 2023 Germany had **40 million patient transports by taxi and rental car** and **5.6 million by specialised patient
transport**, plus **7.8 million rescue trips and emergency-doctor vehicle trips**. Patient transport is therefore highly
significant for statutory health and long-term-care insurance [1].

This paper aims to motivate the relevant actors to start implementing the process in their own area and to support it [2].
The participants jointly developed approaches and recommendations.

**The discussions showed in particular:**

1. **The e-prescription of patient transport needs a clear framework.** It should be checked whether the legal framework
   must be changed. The same applies to legal rules for **proxies/representatives** (*Vertreterregelungen*). Possible
   changes to the **G-BA guideline** for patient transport and to the **Federal Framework Agreement**
   (*Bundesmantelvertrag*) should be addressed early. A working group of the self-governing bodies (*Selbstverwaltung*)
   should start soon on the content and technical requirements.
2. **The e-prescription should run through a "Fachdienst"** (central specialist service). gematik should be commissioned to
   build a Fachdienst for the e-prescription (*eVerordnung*) and define the necessary interfaces. The self-governing bodies
   should define its content. Existing or in-development solutions for other e-prescriptions, and future solutions of
   **Telematics Infrastructure 2.0**, should be taken into account.
3. **The digital process should make selecting, carrying out and billing patient transport easier.** **Transport companies
   should get digital access to the e-prescription data without needing a connection to the Telematics Infrastructure**,
   so that they can provide suitable vehicles. Access must be secure and compliant with data protection law. The
   self-governing bodies must also define a **user-oriented digital service confirmation** (*digitale Leistungsbestätigung*).
4. **The digital process can be expanded step by step after launch.** In later stages, health insurers could help their
   insured choose suitable transport via digital solutions. **Process status updates** could also be added, showing
   transparently where an e-prescription is in the approval procedure.

## What digital applications offer (p. 3)

German healthcare and nursing care still use a lot of paper. Every day, people fill in, process and approve many, sometimes
complex, applications. Digitalisation can make application processes simpler, faster and more transparent: applications
become easier to understand, process and track.

Since March 2023 the digitalisation strategy *"Gemeinsam Digital"* has guided digital transformation in care. Most
application and approval procedures can be digitised. To realise the potential, it is **not enough to simply "rebuild"
existing processes electronically**. They must be rethought across disciplines and implemented in a forward-looking way,
using the practical knowledge of the people involved. On behalf of the Federal Ministry of Health, gematik coordinates and
moderates this joint work.

### How the working group rethought the prescription of patient transport

The group had **17 participants** from all areas of German healthcare: representatives of patients, care providers,
insurers, transport companies and industry. Together their perspectives gave a well-founded, practical picture of the
existing processes.

People in Germany use **more than 50 million patient transports per year**. The paper prescription consists of a
**double-sided form, "Muster 4"** [3]. Because it is needed so often, it causes considerable bureaucracy for prescribers,
insurers and transport companies. In German doctors' practices alone it caused **4.1 million working hours in 2020**, and the number is rising [4].

## (p. 4)

Doctors issue Muster 4 for medically necessary trips [5]. The form distinguishes several **case constellations** that
establish the entitlement. Basically there are trips that **do not need approval** (*genehmigungsfrei*) and trips the
**insurer must approve** (*genehmigungspflichtig*). The KBV (National Association of Statutory Health Insurance Physicians)
provides fill-in aids for practices [6].

- **No approval needed:** medically necessary trips for **inpatient treatment** (including pre- and post-inpatient care).
  Trips to **outpatient** treatment may only be prescribed under certain conditions (e.g. specific severe-disability
  markers or certain care levels). For these groups, **taxi or rental-car** trips need no approval.
- **Approval needed:** trips with a **patient transport ambulance (KTW)** for those severely disabled and highly
  care-dependent groups; the medical necessity is checked. Approval is also needed for patients with **mobility
  impairments without a marker or care level** whose outpatient treatment lasts **at least six months**, and for patients
  who need **high-frequency treatment over a long time** (e.g. dialysis, radiotherapy, parenteral chemotherapy). The same
  applies to patients who absolutely need a KTW, because they must be transported professionally or need medical attention during the trip.

Insured persons must pay a **legal co-payment** (*Zuzahlung*) unless exempt [7]. **After the trip, the insured person
confirms it with a signature on the back of Muster 4.** Finally, the transport company confirms the trips, bills the
insurer and invoices the co-payment to the patient.

Apart from prescriptions, insured persons can apply to have the costs of **public transport or private cars** reimbursed,
with proof. **Ambulance or helicopter** transport is requested via the emergency number **112**.

The working group **focused on the prescription** of patient transport. **Reimbursement applications and rescue transports were excluded.**

## (p. 5)

The paper-based process with many participants is **error-prone**. All relevant information, from the medical necessity to
the proof of completed trips, is on **one single form**. If Muster 4 is lost, a new one must be issued. Some prescribers
find the case constellations hard to understand. Errors include **wrong addresses, unclear co-payment exemption status, or
wrongly placed crosses**. According to the experts, such errors are often **only noticed by the transport provider at
billing time** and cause lengthy correction loops. Manual review of approval-requiring trips also takes time.

The group estimates that a digitally redesigned prescription can **significantly reduce processing time, increase
transparency and reduce errors**. It estimates that **about 10% of paper prescriptions contain errors**, and that
correcting one causes **about half an hour of extra work**.

To avoid these gaps in future, the group defined requirements beyond the prescription itself:

- Practice and hospital software (**PVS/KIS**) should add **fill-in hints, logical links, plausibility checks and warnings**.
- If possible, the insured person's **co-payment exemption should be stored digitally**.
- **Queries and error corrections should be possible digitally.**
- Digital billing between transport providers and insurers should be able to **reuse the prescription data easily**.

## How a digital process meets everyone's needs (p. 6)

The jointly identified error-proneness shows clear potential for improvement, which the participants turned into concrete
requirements for a future target process, aiming at more **process reliability, transparency and usability**. Figure 1
(p. 8) shows the key points, from prescription to billing.

In the redesigned process, the need for a trip is still determined in direct connection with the medical service and the
patient's condition.

**(1) Issuing the e-prescription** is digital. The choice of vehicle follows medical necessity, supported by aids like
**dropdown menus**. **If a KTW is chosen, a medical justification is a mandatory field.** The process should also allow for
the need changing during treatment. The e-prescription is **pre-filled with existing patient data** and requires choosing
the specific **destination address**. Before completion, the PVS or KIS runs a **plausibility and completeness check**. The
system should also indicate **whether the trip needs approval or not**. Central to this is the **active involvement of the
patient**, who is always free to decide whether they want transport at all. The e-prescription is then sent to the
**Fachdienst** (still to be built).

**(2a) Trips not needing approval:** the patient receives the e-prescription directly when it is issued, **e.g. via an app**.
At the same time the insurer is informed that it was sent to the Fachdienst.

**(2b) Trips needing approval:** insurers want to be informed about the e-prescription stored on the Fachdienst so they can
retrieve it and check eligibility. The **decision is reported back to the Fachdienst, ideally in real time**. The patient
receives the decision **analogue (e.g. letter) or digitally**, according to preference.

## (p. 7)

**In both cases the patient receives a "key"** (*Schlüssel*), **for example an order number or a QR code**, which can be
passed to a transport company. **The patient decides which provider gets access to the e-prescription**, supported if
needed by the practice, hospital staff or insurer (e.g. through a provider directory still to be created).

**(3) Request to the transport company** [8]: the patient passes the key directly to the chosen provider, so that all
relevant trip information can be **securely retrieved from the Fachdienst**. Transport companies should be able to
**view the relevant trip information before accepting the job**, so they can check early whether they can do it.

**(4) Trips:** after successful data retrieval and acceptance, the trips are carried out. This ensures all relevant
information is available and transport is organised appropriately and efficiently. **The patient confirms the completed
trips digitally to the transport company.** Unless exempt, the patient pays the legal co-payment directly to the company.

**(5) Billing:** after the trip(s) and the patient's confirmation, billing takes place between the transport company and
the insurer, possibly via billing service providers. **The patient's confirmation proves to the insurer that the service was actually provided.**

## From the redesigned process to practice (p. 9)

According to the group, these aspects are decisive for success.

**The Fachdienst for the e-prescription of patient transport needs a clear framework.**
The group recommends implementing the process technically as a **workflow solution with a Fachdienst at gematik**. This
requires adjusted regulations. The Federal Ministry of Health is asked to check how laws could be adapted. Experience with
the **eRezept** and the **e-prescription of home nursing care** (*häusliche Krankenpflege*) can serve as a reference. The
Ministry should also check whether **proxy rules for redeeming the prescription (e.g. by relatives or carers)** need to be
regulated by law.

gematik should be commissioned to **build and maintain the Fachdienst and define the interfaces**, reusing technical
solutions of existing or in-development e-prescriptions where possible. The self-governing bodies want to be actively involved.

The **G-BA patient transport guideline** would need adjustments initiated by the Federal Framework Agreement partners,
including checking whether **individual prescriptions for high-frequency treatments of mobility-impaired people could be
replaced by multi-prescriptions** (*Mehrfachverordnungen*). Current legislation on emergency care should also be considered.

## (p. 10)

**An e-prescription reduces errors and effort through technical solutions.**
The group recommends that the KBV and the GKV-SV (National Association of Statutory Health Insurance Funds) check Muster 4
for **redundancies**, especially regarding the assessment of medical need, and jointly decide **which data the
e-prescription uses**. **Fewer free-text fields** (e.g. dropdowns, automated mouse-over hints) and **plausibility checks**
in PVS and KIS can reduce errors [9]. Timely, compliant implementation by software vendors should be ensured by
certification. **Electronic correction options** for faulty e-prescriptions should be developed.

**Digital support eases selection, execution and billing of patient transport.**
- The group recommends that **transport companies get digital access to the necessary data** (including **permanent
  approvals, series trips, co-payment exemption**) so they can provide the right vehicle. This **avoids empty trips and
  billing problems**. The Federal Framework Agreement partners should talk early with transport companies and with makers
  of **fleet and billing software**. **User-friendly technical access options should be chosen that do not necessarily
  require a TI connection.** A **selection mechanism for suitable transport companies and vehicles** should also be identified.
- **Proof of completed trips should also be digital.** Suitable technical solutions should be identified, and the scope of
  a **digital service confirmation** described.
- Extending digital billing for transport companies beyond today's options should be examined.
- Insurers should support patients in choosing transport, e.g. with **lists of contract partners**. Later, insurers could
  provide **digital platforms or provider directories**.

## From foundations to implementation (p. 11)

The group estimates **about three years** for implementation, since changing laws and guidelines takes time. Work on the
content and technical prerequisites should therefore **start soon, before the law changes**.

Implementation can be sped up by reusing existing or in-development solutions, learning from **pilot projects** on patient
transport and travel cost reimbursement, and incorporating **TI 2.0** innovations.

When defining **datasets, the process for determining approval requirements and vehicle suitability, digital retrieval of
data by transport companies, and requirements for software vendors**, **user interests should lead**. The self-governing
bodies should talk early with all other participants and with **patient representatives**.

A **trial phase** should test the e-prescription in practice. The rollout should be accompanied by communication to users.

## Recommendations and conclusion (pp. 12–14)

The group is convinced that the digital process enables more transparency, shorter processing times and user orientation.
Concepts may change due to announced legislation.

**1. The e-prescription of patient transport needs a clear framework**
- The Ministry of Health should check whether the law must change. If so, align it with the **e-prescription of home nursing care**.
- The Ministry should check **legal proxy rules** (e.g. relatives, carers) for redeeming e-prescriptions across the board.
- The self-governing bodies should tackle changes to the G-BA guideline and Federal Framework Agreement early.
- A self-governance working group should start on content and technical requirements **before** the new framework,
  in dialogue with gematik, practice-software vendors, industry-software vendors and transport companies, using pilot project experience.

**2. The e-prescription should run through a Fachdienst**
- gematik should be commissioned to **build a Fachdienst** and define interfaces.
- The self-governing bodies define the **content and information model**, including fill-in aids (plausibility checks,
  dropdowns), with certification of software vendors.
- Reuse existing e-prescription solutions (**eRezept, eVO DiGA, eVO home nursing care**) and future **TI 2.0** solutions.
- When issuing, the **co-payment exemption should be taken into account by reading the eGK** (health card).
- A **uniform, ideally automated response** from insurers on approval or rejection, e.g. via the insurer's own app or the **TI-Messenger**.
- Allow entry of the **site number** (*Betriebsstättennummer*). Insurers get access via the Fachdienst to both trip types.
  **Traceable electronic correction via the Fachdienst** should be possible.

**3. The digital process should ease selection, execution and billing**
- **Transport companies should get digital access to e-prescription data without a TI connection.**
- Possible access routes: **WANDA** (*Weitere Anwendungen für den Datenaustausch*, i.e. additional applications for data
  exchange in the TI) [10], **platform solutions, or insurer websites**. For website access, providers should identify
  themselves via the **Authenticator in a browser**. TI 2.0 options should be considered.
- Access must be **data-protection compliant and secure**, e.g. when commissioning the company, via an **identification
  code of the prescription, certain insured master data, QR or Data-Matrix codes**.
- For **permanent approvals or series trips**, **several transport companies** should be able to access the data, to use capacity efficiently.

**(p. 14)**
- The self-governing bodies must define a **user-oriented digital service confirmation**, e.g. an **"eLeistungsbestätigung"**
  or via the existing **TP5 procedure** [11].

**4. The digital process can be expanded step by step**
- Insurers could later support insured persons in choosing transport through **digital provider directories and platforms**.
- Later stages could check **automatic retrieval of severe-disability markers**, e.g. via exchange with the pension
  offices (*Versorgungsämter*), the **ePA** (electronic patient record) or updated insured-master-data management.
- **Status updates on the e-prescription's progress** could be added, based on eRezept experience.
- **Interfaces for billing service providers** should be considered from the start. They could also offer **apps for
  service confirmation** or lists of available providers. **Online marketplaces for trip brokering** should be involved.
- **If apps are used**, on both the insured person's and the transport company's side they must meet the **special
  security requirements for social data**.
